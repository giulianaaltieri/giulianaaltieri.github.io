Companion App

Putting the vehicle back at the heart of a car-management app.

Role
UX logic & full UI
Sector
Automotive · Mobile
Client
International automotive client
Status
In development

Final UI: Vehicle, Home and Navigation are now the three pillars of the app.

Problem

Vehicle data — the core of the app — was hidden in a small, vaguely named widget.

Solution

Rename it “Vehicle”, make it a main section and rebuild the home around the car.

Result

A clearer structure that moved into development.

01

Context

A mobile companion app for an international automotive client. One account can be linked to one or more vehicles, and the app brings together vehicle status, navigation and everyday information.

I joined to design the UI — and soon realised the structure underneath needed to change first.

02

The problem

The app is built around the vehicle, yet the most important vehicle information was the hardest thing to find. The home mixed purely informative data with a grid of small widgets, all with the same visual weight even though they mattered very differently: Weather sat right next to Health.

Health was in fact the core of the app: tire pressure and monitoring, engine status and every other diagnostic category. But it was hidden in a small widget, and its name did not tell users what was inside.

03

What I did

  1. 01

    A name that says what it is

    I renamed “Health” to “Vehicle”, so users know immediately where to find their car’s status.

  2. 02

    From hidden widget to main destination

    I moved Vehicle into the bottom navigation bar, next to Home and Navigation: the car’s status is now one tap away from any screen.

  3. 03

    A home built around the car

    I redesigned the home with a render of the vehicle as its visual anchor and a clearer hierarchy of information.

  4. 04

    Multi-vehicle, made visible

    Because an account can hold more than one vehicle, I added a page indicator under the car: users understand at a glance that they can swipe between vehicles.

Outcome

The new information architecture and UI moved into development, where the app is today.
